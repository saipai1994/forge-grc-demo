# Antler India application kit (draft for you to edit and paste)

Apply at https://www.antler.co/location/india (or https://www.antler.co/apply). Items in [brackets] are yours to fill. Do not submit anything you cannot stand behind in an interview.

## 1. What I could and could not confirm (13 Oct 2026)

I could not open antler.co from my environment, so none of this comes from their live pages. From search results:

- The residency is a **full-time commitment**, and Antler has said it welcomes **solo founders** and early teams, with or without an idea.
- Selection is by online form, then about two or three interviews on motivation, resilience and founder potential, then an offer.
- Reported terms **conflict**: third-party profiles say about ₹2 crore for about 9% equity for the standard India residency; the India AI Residency advertised up to ₹4 crore. Treat all of this as unconfirmed.
- Antler's apply page listed cohorts in other cities and no India dates when I searched. India dates may be posted on the India page or announced separately.

**Before you do anything else:** open the India page, read the current dates, terms and the form questions, and compare them with the answers below.

## 2. Check these first (they can stop an application)

1. **Employment, non-compete and IP.** Check any current or former employer contract (including IBM or an IBM partner) for non-compete, moonlighting and IP-assignment clauses that could touch this product. Antler will ask. If unsure, get a short legal opinion.
2. **Full-time availability** for the length of the residency, and which city.
3. **Equity terms.** Read the exact percentage, vesting, what the cheque buys, and what happens if you leave the cohort.
4. **What Antler can see.** The GitHub repository is currently public. Decide whether to make it private before sharing a demo link (Repo Settings, Danger Zone).
5. **Be ready to say what is real.** Built: a working product (demo with sample data and a deployable server). Not done: customers, pilots, hosting, penetration test, certifications, any revenue.

## 3. Draft answers

**One-liner.** Forge GRC is risk, compliance and audit software for regulated firms, starting with Indian NBFCs and banks, that is live in weeks, not quarters, because it ships with built-in regulatory libraries.

**The problem.** Mid-sized regulated firms manage risk, controls, audits and regulatory change in spreadsheets and email, while enterprise GRC suites take months to implement and sit in the top cost band. New rules (RBI IT governance, DPDP, SEBI cyber framework) raise the evidence burden every year.

**What I have built.** A working GRC platform: risks, controls, obligations, audits, policies, vendors, a regulatory-change log, board reporting, drill-down from any number to its records, a vendor questionnaire portal, an anonymous speak-up channel, sign-in with MFA and SSO, and a deployable server. It ships with a built-in library of [278] frameworks and about [2,000] requirements across India, ANZ, the Middle East, Europe, the Philippines, the USA and Canada, plus sample organisations for 17 industries. [Demo link]

**What is not done (say this plainly).** No paying customers or pilots, nothing hosted, no penetration test or certification. The library was written from secondary sources and needs expert review.

**Why me.** [About two years working with IBM OpenPages as ROLE. Describe what you personally did and which kinds of organisations you saw, in general terms only. I am independent of IBM and use no IBM material.]

**Who is the customer and why will they pay.** Compliance heads and CROs at mid-sized NBFCs and co-operative or small finance banks who need audit-ready evidence and cannot afford a multi-month implementation. My hypothesis, to be tested: they will pay [₹8 to 24 lakh a year] for something live in weeks.

**Validation so far.** [Number] conversations with [roles]. Honest default if none yet: "None yet. Before the cohort I will run 20 discovery interviews and ask for three paid pilots."

**Competition.** Enterprise suites (IBM OpenPages, ServiceNow, Archer), mid-market tools (LogicGate, AuditBoard) and Indian specialists (eQomply, Scrut, Dcomply). I have not done hands-on comparisons yet; that is on my plan.

**What I want from Antler.** (1) A co-founder or first hire for sales and BFSI relationships. (2) Introductions to compliance heads at NBFCs and banks for design partners. (3) Help with hosting, a penetration test and an ISO 27001 path. (4) Fundraising guidance after the first paid pilots.

**Biggest risks.** Long bank sales cycles; trust for a solo founder; accuracy of regulatory content; competition from Indian specialists. My responses: start with smaller NBFCs, use advisers as a channel, add expert review, and compete on speed and breadth.

**Ninety-day plan.** Weeks 1 to 4: 20 interviews, a hosted pilot environment, a penetration test booked. Weeks 5 to 8: three design-partner pilots, paid. Weeks 9 to 12: first paid conversions, a referenceable case study, expert review of the RBI and DPDP content.

## 4. Likely interview questions, and honest answers

- **Why you, and why alone?** Domain insight from OpenPages plus a built product; I am open to a co-founder and want one for sales.
- **Why would a bank trust a one-person company?** It should not yet, so I start with smaller NBFCs, add a penetration test and certification plan, and use a respected adviser.
- **What do you know that incumbents miss?** [Your specific, non-confidential examples of pain you saw. Never client names or IBM material.]
- **Is the content correct?** It is starter content from secondary sources; each entry has a review panel and a verify note; I plan expert review of the most-used items.
- **How did you build this alone so fast?** I used AI assistance and I am direct about it. [Your account of what you decided, directed and tested.]
- **What happens if no one pays?** I run the 20 interviews and a paid-pilot offer; if fewer than 3 of 10 accept, I change the segment or offer before spending more.
